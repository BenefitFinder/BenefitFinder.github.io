The Aged, Blind, or Disabled (ABD) Cash Program provides cash assistance to adults who have very low income and meet age, blindness, or disability requirements. This program is meant to support people who are unable to work while they pursue federal disability benefits (SSI/SSDI).

DSHS may refer approved clients to the Housing and Essential Needs (HEN) program if eligible.

Below you can learn about the full eligibility rules, required verification, and what documents to expect when applying.

### Additional Notes on Eligibility

Here’s a quick checklist commonly used to determine whether someone is likely eligible for ABD:

- You live in Washington  
- You are 65+, blind, or have a long-term disabling condition lasting 12+ months  
- Your income is very low (typically under ~$450–$700/mo depending on circumstances)  
- You have limited resources (usually under $6,000)  
- You are a U.S. citizen or qualifying legal immigrant 
- You are **not** currently receiving SSI (if you later get SSI, ABD ends)  
- You agree to participate in the SSI application process if required  

ABD is a “last resort” program, so DSHS will check whether you qualify for other programs such as TANF or RCA first.

### Examples of Documents You May Need

<div class="faq-section">

  <details>
    <summary>1. Proof of Identity</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>State ID or driver’s license</li>
      <li>U.S. passport</li>
      <li>Birth certificate</li>
      <li>Tribal ID</li>
      <li>Any government-issued document with your name and photo</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>2. Social Security Number (SSN)</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Social Security card</li>
      <li>Any document showing your SSN (pay stub, SSA letter, tax form)</li>
      <li>If you do not have an SSN, DSHS will require proof you have applied for one</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>3. Proof of Age</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Birth certificate</li>
      <li>Driver’s license or state ID</li>
      <li>Passport</li>
      <li>Any official document listing your date of birth</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Only required if you are applying based on being age 65 or older.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>4. Proof of Disability or Blindness</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>SSA disability award letter (if applicable)</li>
      <li>Medical records, doctor’s statements, or hospital records</li>
      <li>Physical, mental, or psychiatric evaluations</li>
      <li>Other medical documents showing the condition is expected to last 12+ months</li>
    </ul>
    <p>DSHS may request you complete additional evaluations with a contracted provider.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>5. Proof of Income</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Pay stubs (if recently working)</li>
      <li>Employer statements</li>
      <li>SSA, SSI, or VA benefit letters</li>
      <li>Unemployment benefit statements</li>
      <li>Pension, retirement, or annuity statements</li>
      <li>Bank statements showing deposits</li>
      <li>Self-employment records, invoices, or tax returns</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>6. Proof of Resources (Assets)</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Bank statements</li>
      <li>Vehicle title/registration (if applicable)</li>
      <li>Life insurance policy information</li>
      <li>Property tax or ownership documents</li>
      <li>Any documents showing savings, funds, or property</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>7. Proof of Immigration or Citizenship Status</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Permanent Resident Card (Green Card)</li>
      <li>I-94 or USCIS documents</li>
      <li>Work Authorization Card</li>
      <li>Proof of refugee or asylee status</li>
      <li>Certificate of Naturalization or U.S. passport</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Only required for the person (or people) applying for benefits.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>8. Washington Residency & Living Situation</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Lease, rental agreement, or mortgage statement</li>
      <li>Utility bills</li>
      <li>Letter from a landlord or roommate</li>
      <li>Letter from a shelter, agency, or case worker</li>
      <li>Any document showing you live in Washington</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>9. Household Expenses (May affect other programs such as HEN)</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>Rent or mortgage payment</li>
      <li>Utility bills</li>

      <li>Childcare or dependent care costs</li>
      <li>Property taxes or homeowner’s insurance</li>
    </ul>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>10. Disability Determination Forms (If requested)</summary>
    <ul>
      <li>DSHS medical evaluation forms</li>
      <li>Psychological or physical evaluation referrals</li>
      <li>Forms related to SSI/SSDI application process</li>
    </ul>
    <p>DSHS may require you to apply for SSI. ABD benefits end if you begin receiving SSI.</p>

### Notes on Benefit Amount
- Most clients receive around $450 per month in cash assistance.  
- Payments are typically issued via debit card or direct deposit.  
- Amounts do not vary widely by household size.

<br>

### Program Disclaimer
The ABD Cash Program is administered by the Washington State Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS). Rules may change based on state law or federal disability requirements so it is always best to confirm requirements with their office or website.
